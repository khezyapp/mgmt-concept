# 08 — Glossary and Frameworks

Short definitions, plus the confusion each term usually causes. Read this when a
document uses a word as if everyone already agreed on it.

---

## 1. Direction and planning

| Term | Meaning | Don't confuse with |
|---|---|---|
| **Vision** | The future state an organisation wants to create. One sentence, no numbers required. | A goal or a target |
| **Mission** | What the organisation does, for whom, and why it exists today. | Vision (future) |
| **Goal** | The broad change or result intended for a target group. | Objective (measurable step) |
| **Objective** | A specific, measurable result to be achieved by a date. | Strategy (the path) |
| **Strategy** | The chosen path to overcome a major bottleneck standing between you and the goal. A choice, including what not to do. | Tactic, plan, or target |
| **Strategic pillar** | A broad area of focus that groups strategies and resources. | A project |
| **Tactic** | A short-term move within a strategy. | Strategy |
| **Theory of change** | The causal logic: if we do A, then B changes, because C. | A work plan |
| **Strategic framework** | The architecture that sets vision, objectives, pillars, scope, and guardrails. | An action plan |
| **Scope** | What is included and excluded. | Vision |
| **Guardrail** | A boundary that must not be crossed while pursuing a goal (e.g., data protection). | A target |
| **Mandate** | The authority to act, usually legal or delegated. | Capability |
| **Delegation** | Transfer of authority to act on someone's behalf, with accountability retained upward. | Abandonment of responsibility |

**Key distinction:** a strategy is a *choice of path*, an objective is a *result*, and a
plan is a *schedule*. Documents often blur them; you must not.

## 2. Governance documents

| Term | Meaning | Answers | Mandatory? |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Policy** | Executive mandate setting principles, boundaries, roles, and compliance rules. | What must or must not be done? | Yes |
| **Standard** | Exact technical or operational requirement that enforces a policy. | What precise spec must be met? | Yes |
| **Procedure / SOP** | Step-by-step instructions for a repeatable process, with roles and records. | Who does what, in what order? | Yes, for the process |
| **Guideline** | Recommended practice or pattern; deviation allowed with reason. | What is the preferred approach? | No |
| **Action plan** | Time-bound tasks with owners, dates, budget, and deliverables. | Who does what, by when? | Yes, once approved |
| **Control** | A measure that prevents, detects, or corrects a risk. | What keeps this safe/correct? | Depends on policy |
| **Record** | Evidence that an activity happened as required. | What proof exists? | Yes |

**External vs internal standard:** ISO 9001, ISO 27001, and similar are *external*
standards. Your organisation adopts them through policy, then writes *internal* standards,
procedures, and records to comply. Do not call an ISO publication "our standard"
without saying which internal document implements it.

**Hierarchy rule:** lower layers may be stricter than higher layers, never looser.
A procedure that contradicts a policy is a defect, even if it is faster.

## 3. Measurement and results

| Term | Meaning | Don't confuse with |
|---|---|---|
| **Output** | What you delivered (portal, training, report). | Outcome |
| **Outcome** | The change in behaviour or performance caused by outputs (faster approvals, more loans). | Impact |
| **Impact** | The longer-term contribution to national or social change (jobs, growth). | Outcome |
| **KPI** | A measure used to judge progress on an objective. | Target |
| **Baseline** | The starting value before intervention, with date and source. | Target |
| **Target** | The value you commit to reach by a date. | Forecast |
| **Milestone** | A significant point in time or delivery checkpoint. | Target |
| **Indicator** | A variable that signals whether a result is happening. | KPI (a selected indicator) |
| **Proxy indicator** | An indirect measure used when the direct one is unavailable. | Official indicator |
| **Disaggregation** | Breaking data down (sex, size, province, sector) to see who is affected. | Segmentation (marketing) |
| **M&E** | Monitoring (ongoing progress) and evaluation (periodic judgement of results). | Audit |
| **Logframe** | A matrix of objectives, indicators, means of verification, and assumptions. | Work plan |
| **OKR** | Objective + key results; a goal-setting method, usually quarterly. | KPI dashboard |
| **Balanced Scorecard** | A measurement framework across financial, customer, process, and learning perspectives. | Performance appraisal |

**Golden rule:** outputs are countable; outcomes require a comparison. "Built a portal"
is an output. "Reduced decision time from 10 days to 2" is an outcome.

## 4. Delivery

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Programme** | A group of related projects managed together for a shared outcome. |
| **Project** | A temporary effort with a defined scope, budget, start, and end. |
| **Portfolio** | The full set of projects and programmes an organisation invests in. |
| **Initiative** | A labelled change effort; may contain one or more projects. |
| **Task** | One assignable unit of work with an owner, due date, and deliverable. |
| **WBS** | Work breakdown structure: hierarchical decomposition of deliverables. |
| **Deliverable** | A tangible output that must be produced and accepted. |
| **Acceptance criteria** | Testable conditions that define "done". |
| **Definition of done** | The team's standard for completed work, including evidence. |
| **RACI** | Responsible, Accountable, Consulted, Informed. One accountable person per activity. |
| **Dependency** | Work or decision that must happen before or alongside yours. |
| **Critical path** | The sequence of tasks that determines the earliest completion date. |
| **Scope creep** | Uncontrolled expansion of work without adjusting time, cost, or quality. |

## 5. Risk, control, and assurance

| Term | Meaning | Don't confuse with |
|---|---|---|
| **Risk** | The effect of uncertainty on objectives (ISO 31000). Usually stated as cause → event → effect. | Issue (already happened) |
| **Risk appetite** | The amount and type of risk an organisation is willing to accept. | Risk tolerance (specific threshold) |
| **Control** | A measure that modifies risk. Preventive, detective, or corrective. | Mitigation plan |
| **Assurance** | Independent confidence that controls work as intended. | Management reporting |
| **Audit** | Systematic, independent examination against criteria. | Evaluation |
| **Compliance** | Meeting laws, regulations, standards, and internal rules. | Conformance (meeting a specified requirement) |
| **Non-conformity** | Failure to meet a requirement. | Observation (weaker signal) |
| **Finding** | Audit conclusion with condition, criteria, cause, effect, and recommendation. | Opinion |
| **CAPA** | Corrective and preventive action to fix a non-conformity and stop recurrence. | Quick fix |
| **Evidence** | Information that supports a conclusion or demonstrates compliance. | Documentation (the container) |
| **Traceability** | Ability to follow a requirement through design, delivery, and evidence. | Lineage (data) |
| **Segregation of duties** | Splitting tasks so no one person controls all steps. | Job rotation |
| **Three lines model** | Management (1st), risk/compliance (2nd), internal audit (3rd). | Three-year plan |

## 6. Governance and institutions

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Governance** | The system by which decisions are made, authority is exercised, and performance is held to account. |
| **Accountability** | Answerability for a result; cannot be delegated away. |
| **Steering committee** | Senior group that approves direction, budget, and escalations. |
| **Secretariat** | Unit that coordinates, consolidates reports, and supports a committee. |
| **Stakeholder** | A person or group affected by or able to affect the outcome. |
| **ToR (terms of reference)** | Document defining purpose, scope, roles, deliverables, and reporting for a body or assignment. |
| **Due diligence** | Investigation before a decision or partnership to identify risks and obligations. |
| **Public–private partnership (PPP)** | Long-term arrangement sharing risk and responsibility between public and private parties. |
| **Co-financing** | Pooling funds from multiple sources (public, private, donors) for a shared financing purpose. |
| **Credit guarantee** | A promise to repay part of a loan if the borrower defaults, reducing lender risk. |
| **M&E framework** | The indicators, data sources, responsibilities, and calendar for monitoring and evaluation. |

## 7. Frameworks: which question does it answer?

| Framework | Focus | Core question | Typical artifacts |
|---|---|---|---|
| **ISO 9001** | Quality management system | How do we consistently meet requirements and improve? | Quality policy, objectives, process maps, procedures, records, CAPA |
| **ISO 31000** | Risk management | How do we identify, assess, treat, and monitor uncertainty? | Risk register, appetite, treatment plan, review |
| **ISO 27001** | Information security management | How do we protect information confidentiality, integrity, availability? | ISMS scope, risk assessment, controls, incident log, audit |
| **ISO 22301** | Business continuity | How do we keep critical services running through disruption? | BIA, continuity plans, exercises |
| **ISO 37301** | Compliance management | How do we meet and evidence our obligations? | Obligation register, compliance controls, monitoring |
| **COSO Internal Control** | Internal control | Are controls designed and operating effectively? | Control matrix, testing, deficiency tracking |
| **COSO ERM** | Enterprise risk | How does risk inform strategy and performance? | Risk appetite, risk profile, scenario analysis |
| **COBIT** | IT governance and management | Does IT deliver value and manage risk? | Governance objectives, process capability, performance |
| **ISO 21500 / PMBOK** | Project management | How do we deliver a project predictably? | Charter, WBS, schedule, risk log, closure |
| **TOGAF** | Enterprise architecture | How do business and technology fit together? | Architecture vision, building blocks, roadmap |
| **Balanced Scorecard** | Strategy measurement | Are we executing the strategy across perspectives? | Objectives, measures, targets, initiatives |

These are **lenses**, not a document hierarchy. Use one or two; do not try to satisfy all
at once. In practice:

- Strategy execution → theory of change + logical framework + KPI tree.
- Control design → COSO + ISO 31000.
- Information systems → ISO 27001 + COBIT + architecture practice.
- Quality/service delivery → ISO 9001 + SOPs.

## 8. Terms that hide a decision

Documents often use neutral words for choices that have consequences. Watch for:

| Wording | What it may hide |
|---|---|
| "appropriate", "suitable", "as needed" | Someone must define criteria later — likely you |
| "in coordination with" | Shared responsibility, unclear accountability |
| "promote", "encourage", "facilitate" | No direct delivery obligation |
| "strengthen", "enhance", "improve" | No baseline, so no measurable target yet |
| "digitalize", "modernize" | A process redesign and data-quality problem, not just software |
| "single window", "interoperable" | Governance and data-sharing agreements, not only APIs |
| "sustainable" | Ongoing operating budget and ownership |
| "measurable results" | Indicators and data sources must be defined |
| "specific action plan" | The document you are expected to write |

When you see these words, write the missing definition and label it `[DESIGN]` or
`[VERIFY]`. That is the translation work.
